# Kristal Farms — Overview

Kristal Farms is a **cold-climate, hydro-powered compute infrastructure** designed to deliver two things at once:

1. **Compute capacity** (hosted in modular “pads”), exported by high-quality fiber.
2. **Local community benefits**, by turning server waste heat into **building heating** and **greenhouse food production**.

The core move is simple: **put the compute in the village near heat users**, not far away near the power source. Instead of building long transmission lines, the project uses local hydro on site and converts waste heat into something useful. Tenants lease **black-box compute pads** (power, cooling, fiber provided), while the village gains low-cost heat and improved connectivity. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

## What it provides (in plain terms)

### For the community
- **Lower-cost heat** for public buildings first (and then homes), with a “heat-first” operating rule. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}  
- **Greenhouse capability** powered by recycled heat (food resilience and local jobs). :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}  
- **Better connectivity**: fiber infrastructure that can also serve local institutions. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}  
- **Transparent reporting** through a public dashboard of outcomes (energy, heat, reliability, community benefit). :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}  

### For compute tenants
- A clean, predictable site: **plug-ready pads** with utilities provided up to the boundary.
- A strict **black-box tenancy model**: the host can monitor infrastructure health, but cannot inspect tenant data or internal operations. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}  
- Clear operating constraints: heat-first rules and environmental compliance are part of the deal. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}  

---

## How it works (conceptual view)

1. **Local hydro powers the site** using a short connection to a village substation (avoiding long new transmission corridors). :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}  
2. **Compute runs in modular pads** (tenants bring hardware/software; the site provides the utilities). :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}  
3. **Waste heat is captured** and routed to local heat sinks (public buildings first, then homes, plus greenhouse and thermal storage). :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}  
4. **Work is exported by fiber** with monitoring focused on availability/latency and separation between tenant traffic and community traffic. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

## The non-negotiables (what makes this “governable infrastructure”)

### Heat-first operations
Compute must not quietly cannibalize the civic purpose. The operating hierarchy is:
**reuse → store → reject**, with community heating prioritized. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

### Black-box tenancy
The host provides utilities and physical security **up to the pad boundary**, and monitors only infrastructure metrics—not tenant content. :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}

### Public accountability
A small, stable set of metrics is published to make performance legible:
PUE, WUE (~0 by design), useful heat delivered, HUF, diesel avoided, and uptime/occupancy (plus network and community benefit indicators). :contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13}

### Inclusive governance
Four standing bodies are defined:
- **Project Council** (overall strategy and dispute resolution) :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}  
- **Heat Committee** (seasonal heat priorities, HUF review) :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}  
- **Environment Committee** (environmental compliance and incident review) :contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}  
- **Kristals Council** (public-interest knowledge commons program, topic selection, validation) :contentReference[oaicite:17]{index=17}  

> Note: **Kristal Farms** is infrastructure. **Kristals** are verifiable knowledge artifacts (technology). Farms can *host* compute that helps produce public-interest outputs, but the concepts remain separate.

## Phased rollout (why this is staged)

Phase 1 proves the basics: compute running, useful heat flowing, data exported by fiber; with thermal storage and greenhouse readiness. :contentReference[oaicite:18]{index=18}  
Scaling happens only after go/no-go criteria are met (reliable fiber, safe commissioned power, functioning heat reuse, signed agreements, seated governance, and a public dashboard). :contentReference[oaicite:19]{index=19}
