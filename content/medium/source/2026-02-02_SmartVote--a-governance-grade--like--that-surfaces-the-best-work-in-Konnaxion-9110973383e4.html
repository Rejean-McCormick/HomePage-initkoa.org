SmartVote: a governance-grade “like” that surfaces the best work in Konnaxion

Most platforms rely on a single, default quality signal: likes.

That works until scale and complexity arrive. When every endorsement counts the same, visibility starts rewarding what’s loud, viral, or coordinated — not what’s accurate, useful, or well-founded. Traditional endorsement systems tend to favor popularity over quality and are vulnerable to misinformation dynamics.

Konnaxion’s answer is SmartVote: a system-wide signal layer that can rank and select almost anything — ideas, solutions, plans, resources, art — while keeping participation broad and outcomes explainable. It aims to surface what’s most endorsed by relevant expertise, not merely what’s most popular overall.

This is the key shift: in a collective intelligence ecosystem, discovery is governance. What rises determines what gets reused, cited, learned, funded, adopted, and built.

SmartVote in one sentence

SmartVote is a universal endorsement + decision mechanism that stores both baseline and expertise/ethics-weighted readings of support, so Konnaxion can elevate the best content and make better choices without collapsing into pure popularity.

The “universal” part matters: SmartVote isn’t only a ballot box for final decisions. It’s a platform-wide primitive that can attach to almost any object in the ecosystem, making everything more “discoverable by merit.”

Why “likes” fail as soon as quality matters

A like button answers one question: “did someone react positively?”

But communities often need a different question answered first:

“Is this good — according to people who can actually evaluate it?”

On technical, scientific, educational, civic, or safety-sensitive topics, generic popularity can be actively misleading. The system you’re designing addresses this by distinguishing:

  • the topology of opinions (what is broadly liked), and
  • the topology of credibility (who has demonstrated competence and integrity in the relevant domain).

SmartVote is the mechanism that lets Konnaxion use both topologies — without turning credibility into hidden authority.

Two-layer architecture: credibility vs tally

SmartVote becomes governable because it is paired with a separate credibility layer:

  • EkoH is the canonical registry + audit backbone for expertise indicators and ethical multipliers.
  • SmartVote is the canonical record of votes/endorsements and results: it stores each vote in both raw_value and weighted_value, with modalities and aggregated outcomes, and acts as the “single source of voting truth.”

This separation matters because discovery and ranking are power. If credibility logic and results are fused into one opaque score, you can’t audit it, contest it, or govern it.

Domain-bounded expertise: the inclusion strategy

The most important design choice is that expertise is domain-bounded, not global.

In your model, each person has a separate weight per domain (economics, ecology, engineering, etc.). Competence in one area does not automatically boost influence elsewhere, preventing undue sway outside one’s competency.

When a proposal — or any content object — is submitted, it is tagged with relevant domains, and only the matching domain weights are applied for the weighted reading.

That domain-boundedness is what keeps the system inclusive:

  • Most people are knowledgeable in something (local context, lived experience, practice, proven contribution).
  • The system can recognize that knowledge where it’s relevant.
  • Nobody automatically becomes a universal authority.

In short: everyone can matter somewhere; nobody matters everywhere by default.

How expertise is determined (not vibes)

A SmartVote system only earns trust if “expertise” is grounded in evidence.

Your domain-bounded model uses multi-source merit scoring: weights are derived from multiple evidence sources — credentials, professional experience, peer-reviewed outputs/awards, prediction/task performance, peer endorsements, and on-platform reputation.

Two governance guardrails make this workable:

  1. Caps and composition rules
    Each source contributes only a portion of the overall score, with caps so no single factor dominates; contributions are logged for transparency with periodic audits/updates.
  2. Relevance stays scoped
    Even high competence in one domain does not automatically transfer influence to unrelated domains; only tagged domains are used for weighting.

This avoids two common failure modes at once: credential monopolies and popularity-as-merit.

Expertise stays current: time decay

Competence shouldn’t fossilize into permanent status.

Your model applies time decay via domain-specific “half-lives,” so older achievements gradually count for less unless refreshed by new validated work.

This is a practical safety and legitimacy feature:

  • it discourages inherited authority,
  • it encourages ongoing contribution,
  • and it keeps credibility aligned with current reality.

The ethical factor: integrity can reduce weight to zero

Expertise alone is not a safety model. Any influence system will attract manipulation.

Your design includes an ethical reliability multiplier (0–3) that can amplify, reduce, or nullify influence — down to 0 — when conduct violates standards (e.g., misinformation patterns, bias, collusion), under a combination of automated checks and human oversight.

This addresses a critical real-world dynamic: sometimes the most knowledgeable actors are also the most capable of manipulating systems. Ethics gating ensures competence cannot override integrity.

Multiple readings: no single hidden score becomes authority

If you compress everything into one final ranking, you create hidden power.

Konnaxion’s approach is to publish at least two readings wherever SmartVote is used:

  • Baseline (raw): what the community supports under equal participation (raw_value).
  • Advisory (weighted): what support looks like when domain-bounded expertise and ethical reliability are applied (weighted_value).

Konnaxion explicitly calls for explainability endpoints (weight breakdown without leaking private data).

This “two readings” structure is the legitimacy mechanism:

  • The system improves signal quality without pretending the baseline doesn’t exist.
  • Disagreements between baseline and advisory become visible and discussable.
  • The platform isn’t silently replacing democratic participation with an algorithm.

SmartVote as a discovery engine: surfacing the best work across Konnaxion

This is the underrepresented core: SmartVote is meant to bring forward the best content across the ecosystem.

The concept stated in your materials is straightforward: weighted endorsements bring quality to the forefront, because content endorsed by knowledgeable and ethical individuals gains higher visibility — so ideas rise due to merit and credibility rather than mere popularity.

That becomes the Konnaxion version of what search ranking did for the web:

Google made it possible to find value without reading everything. Similarly, SmartVote makes it possible for a community to surface the best plans, resources, and creations without drowning in noise — except it is not “most clicked wins.” It is “most endorsed by relevant expertise” (with integrity constraints), while still showing what is popular overall.

Because SmartVote can attach to nearly anything, it can power:

  • ranking and discovery of ideas and solutions,
  • surfacing of educational resources and learning paths,
  • highlighting of high-quality references and research,
  • prioritization of plans and proposals,
  • curation of creative work and cultural artifacts.

It’s “likes,” but governed — and therefore safe enough to become infrastructure rather than a manipulable popularity meter.

How it works end-to-end (in a way that scales beyond voting)

Kintsugi v1 lays out the workflow that makes SmartVote usable as a platform-wide signal, not just an election tool:

  1. Setup: define process, phases, rules, eligible cohorts.
  2. Weight snapshot: pull domain expertise + ethics multipliers, apply privacy, record explanation links.
  3. Casting: record votes/endorsements with raw + weighted values (canonical).
  4. Tally: run the correct tally by modality (approval/rating, ranking/preferential, delegation-enabled).
  5. Publication: publish results plus transparency views.

That same structure applies to discovery:

  • A resource is domain-tagged.
  • Many people endorse it (baseline).
  • Relevant experts endorse it (weighted).
  • If integrity issues arise, weight can drop to 0 (ethics gate).
  • Users see both readings, and the system can rank accordingly.

This is how “best content rises” becomes a governed, repeatable mechanism rather than an emergent accident.

Kintsugi: what it changes (briefly, but materially)

Kintsugi is the program for bringing proven open-source civic apps into Konnaxion under one coherent roof without turning the stack into a monolith, guided by “Mimic vs Annex” so Konnaxion can adopt proven patterns while avoiding “dual truth” platform shells. Concretely, Kintsugi references and/or integrates capabilities and patterns associated with tools like Decidim, Decidim Awesome, and LiquidFeedback, as well as deliberation and participation patterns reflected in Polis, Consider.it, Citizen OS, Loomio, CONSUL Democracy, and DemocracyOS — with optional isolated sidecars such as All Our Ideas, Your Priorities, and OpenSlides. For SmartVote specifically, Kintsugi makes it governance-grade as a platform-wide signal by adding: policy/phase rules (open/close windows, quorum/threshold, tie-break), modality-aware tally backends, explainability endpoints, export/portability bundles, and cross-module embedding so the same SmartVote primitive can power ranking and choice across the ecosystem without duplicating vote logic.

The simplest correct summary

SmartVote is a single mechanism with two roles:

  1. Discovery: surface the best ideas, resources, plans, and creations — by what is most endorsed by relevant domain expertise, constrained by ethical reliability, not by raw popularity.
  2. Decision: support formal choices using the same governed signal, with clear modalities and transparent outputs (raw + weighted).

It is “like” infrastructure that can be trusted — because it is domain-bounded, time-aware, ethics-gated (down to 0), and explainable.

In Konnaxion, “likes” are empowerment.
