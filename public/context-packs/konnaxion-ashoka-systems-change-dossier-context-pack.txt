# Epistemic Integrity, Provenance & Attribution

## Not a truth authority

Konnaxion is not designed to declare truth by institutional fiat.

It is designed to make:

- claims traceable;
- uncertainty visible;
- expertise contextual;
- disagreements legible;
- conclusions contestable;
- revisions inspectable.

## Against two failure modes

### Attention-market failure

Claims gain apparent truth through:

- virality;
- repetition;
- status;
- emotional force;
- distribution power.

### Centralized truth-authority failure

Governments, companies, media, expert institutions, platforms, or AI systems may become practically difficult to challenge even when wrong.

Konnaxion refuses the premise that society must choose between those two conditions.

## Provenance

A claim should be linked to:

- source;
- evidence;
- context;
- assumptions;
- version;
- epistemic state;
- revision history.

## Attribution and human knowledge lineage

The same logic applies to inventions, theories, discoveries, methods, and cultural works.

Knowledge history should be able to preserve:

```text
initial contribution
→ precursor sources
→ formalization
→ experiment / evidence
→ critique
→ validation
→ replication
→ generalization
→ application
```

Different people may have contributed at each stage.

Principle:

> **Aggregation != Attribution Erasure.**

## Important distinctions

> **Attribution != Truth**

Priority does not prove correctness.

> **Attribution != Legal Ownership**

A provenance record is not automatically a patent or copyright determination.

> **Recognition != Sovereignty**

A major contribution does not create permanent authority over a domain.

> **Diffusion != Erasure**

A public good may spread freely while preserving honest lineage.

## A compact political formulation

> **Move from a regime where citizens must choose which authority to believe to one where competing claims can be examined through shared infrastructure for provenance, uncertainty, situated expertise, and contestation.**

# Evidence, Maturity & Limits

## Evidence discipline

The dossier uses four distinct categories.

### 1. Public / inspectable

Code, repositories, documentation, architecture, contracts, essays, and public dossiers.

### 2. Documented architecture

Mechanisms specified coherently but not necessarily fully deployed.

### 3. Partially functional

Implemented slices or components whose full integrated institutional use is not yet demonstrated.

### 4. Scenario / hypothesis

A test case or research proposition.

> **Scenario != Evidence.**

## What the current corpus supports

The public body of work supports serious claims of:

- sustained independent systems construction;
- substantial technical and governance documentation;
- modular system-of-systems architecture;
- explicit epistemic-state modeling;
- provenance and traceability;
- local/offline and bounded-AI design;
- semantic and multilingual architecture;
- mechanisms for deliberation and contextual expertise;
- explicit anti-capture and exit logic;
- pedagogy and capability reproduction;
- public willingness to expose claims to criticism.

## Technical documentation discipline

The kOA-Linux documentation includes automated validation of architectural contracts and boundaries.

A local validation run on 2026-08-17 completed successfully:

```text
python docs/tools/validate_docs.py
```

Result:

```text
validate_docs: pass
```

This is evidence of documentation and architecture discipline.

It is **not** evidence of social impact.

## What is not yet sufficiently proven

The dossier should not claim established evidence for:

- broad institutional adoption;
- national-scale behavior change;
- measured misinformation reduction;
- measured improvement in democratic decisions;
- reliable social mobility through expertise discovery;
- scientific priority disputes resolved in practice;
- multilingual parity at scale;
- independent replication by a second institution.

These are precisely the next proof obligations.

## Strongest current weakness

> **Social validation has not yet caught up with architectural development.**

This is not solved by writing another paper.

It requires independent use.

# The Entrepreneur / Founder Case

## Réjean McCormick

The work should not be evaluated only as an architecture.

The question is whether the architecture reflects a repeatable entrepreneurial pattern in the person who built it.

## Documented trajectory

The available corpus describes a multidisciplinary trajectory crossing:

- humanities;
- electromechanics;
- systems design;
- R&D prototyping;
- organizational transformation;
- software architecture;
- civic governance;
- semantics;
- education;
- cultural and narrative work.

The claim is not that breadth itself is exceptional.

The relevant pattern is **integration under a persistent problem**.

## Modus operandi

> I identify where a system is losing human capability. I cross the disciplinary boundaries necessary to understand the failure. I extract reusable primitives, build mechanisms to reconnect the lost capacities, document them, translate them into teachable forms, and then try to make the resulting infrastructure governable by people other than me.

This pattern is visible across:

- Kristal;
- Konnaxion;
- Koali;
- Orgo;
- EkoH;
- semantic infrastructure;
- UCKK;
- narrative methodology;
- pressure-test and research dossiers.

## Creativity

The work combines:

- social theory with technical contracts;
- provenance with civic legitimacy;
- expertise with democratic equality;
- multilingual semantics with infrastructure sovereignty;
- narrative with formal safeguards;
- modular software with institutional roles;
- public-good logic with exit and fork.

## Adaptability

The architecture institutionalizes revision:

- replaceable components;
- Mimic vs Annex;
- versioning;
- explicit profiles;
- post-mortem;
- contestation;
- fail-closed behavior;
- Scenario != Evidence;
- reopenability.

## Determination

A substantial public corpus has been developed independently rather than as the output of a university, laboratory, company, or funded research program acting as the project owner.

The stronger public case should ultimately include a dated personal chronology of:

- early prototypes;
- major failures;
- pivots;
- resource constraints;
- periods of sustained work;
- moments when the architecture was materially changed.

## Ethical orientation

The most important founder claim is not:

> “I am ethical.”

It is:

> **I am building an infrastructure against manipulation and arbitrary authority, so the same architecture must prevent my own system—and me—from becoming incontestable.**

That is why the work includes:

- fork and exit;
- non-sovereign expert advice;
- non-sovereign AI;
- non-sovereign narrative persona;
- explicit attribution to other contributors;
- independent replication;
- public claims that may be rejected.

## The founder-dependence problem

At present, the founder still carries a large share of the cross-system coherence.

That is a risk, not a virtue.

The next stage must therefore prove:

> **non-founder comprehension → non-founder operation → non-founder teaching → independent reproduction.**

# Pilot & Independent Proof

## Objective

Test a bounded end-to-end social pattern, not the entire ecosystem.

## Minimal complete cycle

```text
real contribution
→ provenance
→ relevant review
→ visible epistemic status
→ structured deliberation
→ real decision / action
→ traceable outcome
→ reusable memory
```

## Candidate domains

- civic / municipal workflow;
- scientific or research collaboration;
- cultural provenance and attribution;
- education and capability reproduction;
- public-interest organizational coordination.

## Core measures

### Articulation

- time contribution → action;
- handoff failures;
- decision-to-execution completion.

### Epistemic integrity

- provenance completeness;
- corrections;
- visible uncertainty;
- challenge/redress time.

### Epistemic mobility

- access to review by prior status;
- recognition of local/non-institutional expertise;
- reviewer diversity.

### Attribution

- contribution lineage retained after aggregation;
- corrections of attribution;
- visibility of precursor work.

### Multilingual dignity

- semantic non-equivalence documented;
- contribution quality across languages;
- access parity.

### Non-domination

- export;
- offline operation;
- bounded AI;
- ability to ignore advisory outputs;
- independent audit;
- credible exit/fork.

### Replication

- non-founder operator;
- non-founder instructor;
- time to deploy a second instance;
- amount of founder intervention required.

## Success condition

> **A partner becomes more capable and can reproduce the capability without becoming dependent on the founder or a mandatory central platform.**

# Public Request for Support — Addressed to Ashoka

**Réjean McCormick**

> **Independent public request. This document does not imply affiliation, endorsement, partnership, or participation by Ashoka.**

## Ashoka,

I am making a public request for support around **Konnaxion and the kOA knowledge-to-action work**.

The request is deliberately narrower than asking you to endorse a platform or validate a worldview.

I am asking Ashoka to examine a systems-change proposition on its substance.

The proposition is this:

> **Modern societies have connected people and information at unprecedented scale, but they have not built the shared infrastructure that allows distributed intelligence to become cumulative collective agency.**

Konnaxion is an attempt to build that missing articulation layer.

It connects:

```text
knowledge
→ provenance
→ contextual competence
→ deliberation
→ judgment
→ decision
→ execution
→ memory
→ future capability
```

while imposing limits designed to prevent that infrastructure from becoming a new center of domination.

Those limits include:

- explicit uncertainty rather than forced certainty;
- provenance rather than source prestige alone;
- contextual expertise rather than permanent expert rank;
- advisory expertise rather than expert sovereignty;
- multilingual semantic governance rather than silent linguistic hegemony;
- local/offline capability rather than mandatory cloud dependence;
- auditability and contestability rather than opaque mediation;
- portability, self-hosting, and fork rather than lock-in;
- preserved attribution rather than erasure through aggregation;
- AI assistance without final civic authority;
- independent reproduction rather than permanent dependence on the founder.

## Why I am addressing this to Ashoka

Ashoka says it looks for a **new idea that changes the pattern in a field**, combined with creativity, entrepreneurial quality, social impact, and ethical fiber.

Ashoka's own systems-change work emphasizes changing the way systems operate—not merely scaling one organization's direct services—and explicitly examines roles, relationships, rules, resources, and results.

That is the correct level at which this work should be evaluated.

Official references:

- https://www.ashoka.org/fr-ca/program/venture-selecting-our-ashoka-fellows
- https://www.ashoka.org/en-us/systems-change
- https://www.ashoka.org/en-us/story/systems-change-high-level-summary

I am therefore not asking for ceremonial recognition.

I am asking for **serious institutional engagement with a body of work that makes a systems-change claim Ashoka is unusually well positioned to interrogate.**

## The request

I am asking Ashoka for one or more of the following forms of support.

### 1. Examine the pattern-change claim

Assess the central proposition at the level Ashoka itself uses:

- What roles change?
- What relationships change?
- What rules change?
- What resources become newly available?
- What results could become structurally different?
- What would have to become common practice for this to count as a genuine new pattern?

If the claim is too broad, identify where it must be narrowed.

If it duplicates an existing pattern, identify the strongest contemporary comparison.

If the novelty lies in the composition rather than individual components, evaluate it at that level.

### 2. Help expose the architecture to independent social proof

The most important gap is not another conceptual document.

It is an independent pilot capable of testing a complete but bounded cycle:

```text
contribution
→ provenance
→ qualified review
→ deliberation
→ decision / action
→ memory
→ reuse
```

Support could take the form of:

- introduction to an appropriate Ashoka Fellow;
- introduction to a civic, scientific, educational, cultural, or institutional partner;
- connection to a practitioner willing to pressure-test the model;
- methodological critique of the proposed impact measures.

### 3. State the missing threshold clearly

I am prepared for the possibility that the current evidence is insufficient.

If so, the most useful support is precision:

> **What evidence would distinguish an unusually developed architecture from an Ashoka-level systems-change trajectory?**

A serious negative answer is more useful than generic encouragement.

## What I am not asking

I am not asking Ashoka to:

- certify a universal truth system;
- endorse every component of kOA;
- accept scenarios as evidence;
- accept technical depth as a substitute for social impact;
- accept founder claims without external reference;
- fund an attempt to build everything at once.

The dossier is explicit about its present boundary:

> **Documented ≠ Implemented ≠ Tested ≠ Socially Validated.**

The work is public-interest infrastructure.

Its claims should be inspectable independently of whether any single organization replies.

Making the request public also creates accountability on my side. I cannot quietly change the proposition depending on the audience. The architecture, the limits, the evidence standard, and the gaps remain visible.

This is the same discipline I have used in two other public dossiers.

> **Do not support this work because it is ambitious. Support it if the pattern, the entrepreneur, the safeguards, and the path to independent proof justify serious engagement.**

## What I believe is already demonstrated

The public corpus supports a serious claim of:

- sustained independent construction;
- multidisciplinary systems engineering;

The goal is not to make Konnaxion a compulsory global platform.

# The Systems-Change Case

## Thesis

Konnaxion is not primarily a product innovation.

The proposed innovation is a change in the social pattern by which groups convert distributed intelligence into collective capacity.

## Current pattern

Many systems remain separated:

```text
people publish
experts advise
citizens discuss
institutions decide
teams execute
archives store
```

The transitions between them routinely lose:

- provenance;
- attribution;
- uncertainty;
- context;
- responsibility;
- dissent;
- learning.

This creates a **conversion loss**.

## Proposed pattern

```text
Distributed human capability
          ↓
Traceable contribution
          ↓
Governed meaning
          ↓
Explicit epistemic status
          ↓
Situated expertise
          ↓
Structured disagreement
          ↓
Legitimate choice
          ↓
Accountable execution
          ↓
Reusable memory
          ↓
Expanded future capability
```

The intellectual center is in the arrows.

## Role changes

### Citizen

```text
consumer / commenter
→ contributor
→ investigator
→ reviewer
→ builder
```

### Expert

```text
prestige authority
→ contextual adviser
```

### Institution

```text
exclusive gatekeeper
→ accountable steward / participant
```

### Platform

```text
attention and dependency engine
→ governable infrastructure
```

### Learner

```text
recipient
→ producer of demonstrated capability
```

### Founder

```text
permanent center

```

## Rule changes

- claims may remain contested;
- uncertainty must remain visible;
- expertise does not transfer automatically across domains;
- informed readings remain advisory;
- provenance follows knowledge;
- attribution survives aggregation;
- AI is bounded;
- local operation is possible;
- exit and fork are legitimate;
- semantic definitions are governable;
- narrative is not evidence.

## Resource changes

Konnaxion treats the following as civic resources:

- provenance;
- contextual competence;
- traceable contribution;
- institutional memory;
- semantic mappings;
- reusable learning;
- portable knowledge;
- coordination capacity.

## Result changes

The intended result is not simply higher participation.

It is a group with greater ability to:

- know;
- challenge;
- decide;
- execute;
- learn;
- reproduce capability.

## Why this is systems change rather than service delivery

The goal is not to grow a single organization until it personally serves every community.

The architecture is designed around:

> **Strong local capability + portable patterns + independent instances + optional federation**

Success therefore means another institution can reproduce the method without permission or permanent operational dependence.

## The social pattern in one sentence

> **Change how groups turn distributed intelligence and participation into cumulative collective capacity.**
